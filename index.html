How to read this

Top chart: net positions. Above zero = net long, below = net short. Commercials usually move opposite to large speculators.

Bottom chart: the COT Index - where today's commercial position sits in its range. Above 80 = near their most bullish, below 20 = near their most bearish. An extreme is a warning, not an entry: ICT waits for price to run liquidity (take out equal highs or lows) before acting on it.

How to read this

Healthy rally: FIIs covering shorts or adding longs while retail sells. Suspicious rally: price rising while FIIs add shorts. The mirror image applies to declines.

Options line = (calls bought - calls sold) - (puts bought - puts sold). Positive = net bullish option exposure.

How to read the dashboard

A top-down routine in four steps.

1

Start from the top

Yearly and Quarterly show the big draw on liquidity. Take Monthly and Weekly trades in that direction; treat counter-trend ideas as quick trades only.

2

Check global money

Hedgers short the dollar and long US bonds = tailwind for Nifty. Hedgers long crude = pressure on the rupee. Your old DXY + yields check, automated.

3

Check who is driving

Is the move led by FIIs (real) or retail (fragile)? FII flips across zero are transitions, not instant signals.

4

Use the levels

"What's next" gives the target liquidity and the invalidation level. Wait for your ICT entry model at those levels; Daily bias is session context only.
